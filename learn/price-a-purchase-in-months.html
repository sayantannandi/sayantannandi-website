Price a purchase in months, not EMIs

A safer new SUV would have cost me 14 months of runway. A house, more than 200.

Most big purchases pass one test: can I afford the EMI on my salary? It is the wrong test, because it assumes the salary never stops.

The months test asks something else. If I buy this, how many months of runway do I give up? Runway is what you can reach, divided by what your household must pay each month.

A few years ago I needed a car. The obvious choice was a brand new SUV, the "safer" one, in line with what my peers drove. About ₹20 lakh. On my numbers then, that was almost 14 months of runway.

A second-hand car of the same build cost about half. Same size, same safety. That one decision kept roughly 7 months of runway in my account. I have not missed the new-car smell once.

Then I ran the same sum on buying a house. Down payment, stamp duty, the EMI added to our monthly costs. It came to more than 200 months of runway.

So I never bought one. We still live on rent, and the money a house would have taken stays invested and keeps compounding. That is my choice for my family, not a rule. A house can be worth it. Many people have reasons that go beyond the sum, and those reasons are real.

The point is to see the price before you pay it. An EMI tells you whether you can pay this month. The months test tells you what the purchase does to your freedom to say no at work for years.

Your turn

Pick the next big thing you plan to buy. How many months of runway does it cost you?

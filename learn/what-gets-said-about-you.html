What gets said about you when you are not in the room

Promotion reviews ask three questions. None of them is about how hard you work.

A promotion is decided in a meeting you do not attend, mostly by people who do not work with you. Your manager speaks for a few minutes. Then the others ask questions.

I sat in those meetings for more than 100 leaders across three continents. Hard work hardly came up, because everyone on the list worked hard.

Three questions came up again and again.

  1. Can anyone say what this person's work changed? A list of what they did was never enough. The room wanted to hear who was better off because of it.
  2. Have they made a call without all the facts? Or do their decisions travel upward as a list of options for someone else to pick from.
  3. Do people outside their team help them? A manager is expected to praise their own people. My read is that a voice from another team counts for more.

Years in the role, hours and ratings were all on the page. They rarely settled anything.

Your manager can only repeat what you have given them. If your updates are a list of activity, then activity is all they have to say when your name comes up.

Your turn

Write the three sentences you would want your manager to say about you in that room. Could they say each one today, with a name and a number in it?

The habit that got you promoted becomes your ceiling

Each level pays for something different. Most people keep doing what the last one paid for.

Most people in their 30s who stall did nothing wrong. They kept doing what had worked.

A career inside a company climbs six steps, and each step pays for something different.

ExpertisePaid to know the answer
ExecutionPaid to deliver the workCarried up: answering every question yourself
Where most people get stuckManagementPaid to deliver through a teamCarried up: fixing the work yourself
StrategyPaid to choose which work mattersCarried up: reporting activity
InfluencePaid to move people you do not manageCarried up: winning the argument
LeadershipPaid to build the people who decideCarried up: deciding everything yourself

Most people I have worked with got stuck on the third step. The trouble is what you carry up. The expert who becomes a lead still answers every question herself. The lead who becomes a manager still fixes the work at night. The manager who is asked about strategy still sends a list of what the team did.

Each of those habits earned the last promotion. That is why they are hard to drop. They feel like the reason you are good at your job.

I sat in talent and promotion reviews for more than 100 leaders. Nobody in those rooms argued about who worked hardest. The question was whether the person was already doing what the next step pays for.

Take Meera, a made-up engineering manager with nine years behind her. She reviews every pull request her team writes, and her team ships clean code. Her director sees a strong lead. He also sees a manager with no time left to say which three of her nine projects should stop.

Your turn

Write down the one thing you still do because nobody does it as well as you. That is likely the habit you carried up. What would you have to trust someone else with, to stop?

Count your money in months

Rupees tell you how rich you feel. Months tell you how long you can say no.

In 2016 I co-founded a company that ran workshops. We had three months of money. It needed twelve. It closed.

The lesson I took from it was to count money in months.

The sum is one division:

Money you can reach ÷ must-pay costs each month = runway in months

Money you can reach is what you could have in your bank account within 30 days: savings, deposits, anything you can turn into cash. Leave out the home you live in and stock that has not vested.

Must-pay costs are what your household pays even in a bad month: rent or EMIs, school fees, insurance, groceries, bills.

A made-up example: ₹18 lakh you can reach, and ₹1.5 lakh of must-pay costs. That is 12 months.

The same ₹18 lakh means very different things in different homes. With costs of ₹3 lakh a month it is 6 months. The rupee figure did not change, and the life it buys halved.

Months are also what you feel at work. With 3 months, a hard conversation with your boss puts your home at risk. With 14, you can afford to have it.

Your turn

Do the division tonight. Then say the number out loud to the other adult in your home, and ask what the two of you would cut first.

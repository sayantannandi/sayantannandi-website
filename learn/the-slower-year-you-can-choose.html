The slower year you can choose

Working less is a real option once lower pay still covers your costs.

Not everyone in their 30s wants the next title this year. A child arrives, a parent falls ill, or you are simply tired. Slowing down for a while is a fair use of what you have built.

It is only a real option if the numbers hold.

Take Shweta, a made-up consulting manager. Her take-home pay is ₹5 lakh a month. She asks to work four days a week for a year, at 80 percent of pay. That is ₹4 lakh a month.

Her household's must-pay costs are ₹3.2 lakh. On ₹4 lakh she still covers them, with ₹80,000 to spare. Her runway does not shrink. It only grows more slowly for a year.

Now change one number. If her costs were ₹4.5 lakh, the same request would cost her ₹50,000 a month from savings. She could still do it, and she would be paying for it in months.

The test is simple. If the lower pay still covers your must-pay costs, a slower year is a choice. If it does not, it is a purchase, and you can price it.

Slowing down also has a cost at work that this sum does not show. A year at four days can delay a promotion. I do not know how to put a number on that. It belongs in the decision all the same.

Your turn

What is the lowest monthly pay that still covers your must-pay costs? How far below your current pay is it?

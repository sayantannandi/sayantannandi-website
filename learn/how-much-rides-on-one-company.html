How much of your life rides on one company

At Flipkart, many colleagues bet their whole future on stock options. I decided not to.

Most of us would never put all our savings into one company. Many of us have done it anyway, because the company is our employer.

When I worked at Flipkart, most people around me held stock options. I had some too. What struck me was how many colleagues had placed their whole future on those options. The house, the children's education, the plan to stop working one day: all of it waited on one company's shares paying out.

My runway was low at the time. I could not afford that bet, and I did not want to make it. So I worked on depending less on two things at once: the salary, and the options. I counted the options as a bonus if they came, not as the plan.

Later, at another large company, I held ESOPs again. I saw the same pattern one level up. Senior leaders spending most of their salary, and counting on the ESOPs to fund their retirement.

Here is why it worries me. If the company has a bad year, everything moves together. The bonus shrinks. The stock falls. The job feels less safe. And that is the year you would most need each of them to hold. This has nothing to do with whether the company is good. Good companies have bad years.

I am not telling you what to hold or sell. That depends on your taxes and your plans, and it is a question for a registered adviser. I am telling you what to count:

  1. The share of your household income that comes from one employer.
  2. The share of what you own, outside your home, that sits in that employer's stock.
  3. The number of people outside the company who might pay for your work.
Your turn

If your company had a bad year, how many of your money streams would fall at the same time?

The pay cut you can afford

A 25 percent cut sounds reckless. Priced in months, it can cost very little.

Take Mitali, a made-up manager at an IT services firm. A product company offers her the work she wants, at lower pay. Her take-home would fall from ₹2.4 lakh a month to ₹1.8 lakh. That is a 25 percent cut, and everyone she asks says no.

Now price it in months.

Her household's must-pay costs are ₹1.9 lakh a month. On the new pay she would be ₹10,000 short each month. Over two years, that is ₹2.4 lakh.

She has 20 months of runway. At ₹1.9 lakh a month, ₹2.4 lakh is a little over one month of it.

So the real question is whether two years in the job she wants are worth about one month of runway. Put that way, most people answer differently than they do to "a 25 percent pay cut".

The sum does not make the decision for her. The new job might not work out. Her costs could rise. It only shows her the true price, and that the price is one she can pay.

The same sum gives a very different answer for someone with 3 months of runway. For them the cut would use up most of what they have. The offer is the same, and the right answer is not.

Your turn

Think of a move you ruled out because of the pay. Work out the monthly shortfall, multiply by 24, and divide by your must-pay costs. How many months is it?

Staying is a choice too

The point of runway was never to leave. It is to stay because you want to.

Most writing about money and careers ends with someone handing in their notice. Most real stories do not.

Take Lokesh, a made-up manager in pharma. He has 22 months of runway. He can name three people outside his company who would take his call. Last month one of them made him an offer.

He turned it down, and stayed where he is.

From the outside nothing changed. He has the same desk, the same boss and the same pay. What changed is why he is there. A year ago he stayed because he could not afford to leave. Now he stays because, having looked at a real offer, he prefers the job he has.

That shows up in how he works. He says what he thinks in meetings. He turns down work that does not matter. His boss has noticed, and it is one reason his next review went well.

This is what I mean by an Optional Career. The career does not become something you leave. It becomes something you chose, with other choices open.

It is also why I do not teach people to quit. Some will. Many will find, once they can afford to go, that they want to stay and do the job differently.

Your turn

If you had two years of runway and one good offer in hand, would you still be in your current job on Monday? What would you do differently there?

Where your last raise went

A raise can make you earn more and last a shorter time. The arithmetic is simple.

A raise feels like progress. Whether it is depends on what it turned into.

Take a made-up household. Before the raise they can reach ₹12 lakh, and their must-pay costs are ₹1,50,000 a month. Twelve lakh divided by one and a half lakh is 8 months of runway. Then take-home pay goes up by ₹30,000 a month.

Path one. Within six months the raise has a home. A car EMI of ₹18,500. A bigger flat at ₹8,000 more rent. Small upgrades of ₹3,500. Must-pay costs are now ₹1,80,000. The same ₹12 lakh lasts about 6.7 months. They earn more, and they can last a shorter time.

Path two. Half the raise becomes new costs. The other ₹15,000 moves out on salary day. Costs are ₹1,65,000. After a year they have added ₹1.8 lakh, so ₹13.8 lakh divided by ₹1.65 lakh is about 8.4 months.

The raise was the same in both. One household ends the year at 6.7 months and the other at 8.4. The gap grows every year, because path one has also raised the bar the next raise must clear.

I know path one from the inside. At 33 my income had nearly doubled in three years, and my runway was eight months.

There is nothing wrong with the car or the holiday. The point is to choose them knowing what they cost you in months.

Your turn

Take your last raise, in rupees a month. List where it goes now. How much of it became a cost you cannot stop next month?

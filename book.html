The idea in one minute

Two numbers describe your working life. Most people track one of them.

Earning Power

What the market will pay you for who you are today.

Exit Power

How long you could decline that income and keep the life your household has chosen, and how many acceptable alternatives you would have.

They multiply. A large income with a few months of runway leaves you with very few real choices, however good the year looks.

What you can do with it

The two numbers buy you a choice. Most people use it in one of three ways.

Where it started

At 33 I had the best year of my career so far. My income rose about 40 percent and I got the title I had wanted. Four months later I sat in a meeting, saw a decision going the wrong way, and said nothing.

That evening I divided what we had by what our household cost each month. The answer was eight. Eight months, after three years in which my income had nearly doubled.

Today that number is above 250. The book is about what I learned between the two.

What the book is not

  • A book about escaping work. I like work.
  • A book about retiring early. That is one use of the idea, and it comes much later.
  • A budgeting book. The money side is simple. The thinking takes longer, because it is tied up with your career.
  • A case for being less ambitious. Push your Earning Power as hard as you can. Only, do not let it be the one thing you work on.

How it connects to what I teach

The skills in the course are the first engine. They raise what the market will pay for you. The second engine is your runway. The book covers both engines, and what the second one does to how you behave at work.

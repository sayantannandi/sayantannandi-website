The philosophy / 4 min read

A successful career should create choices.

Updated 28 October 2026

Leadership skills can strengthen earning power. Exit power connects that income with the freedom to choose.

A larger salary can make a career look more successful. A broader role can make your contribution more valuable. Neither alone tells you how much freedom you have over the next decision.

The philosophy behind this site is simple: build earning power and exit power together.

Earning power: the ability to contribute.

Earning power includes useful skills, sound judgment, relationships and a record of work that others can understand. It grows when you can solve more consequential problems and help other people act well.

The Step Up to Senior Leadership develops the skills for a leadership career. The tests help you identify a practice focus. The course and live cohort help you work on it, including the habits that keep you tied to individual delivery.

Exit power: the ability to choose.

Exit power means having credible alternatives. Financial flexibility can be one part. So can transferable skills, professional relationships, fewer fixed commitments and a realistic view of other paths.

It is not a demand to leave your job. It can support a deliberate decision to stay, negotiate, take time to learn or explore a different role.

Earning power × Exit power = Optionality.

The multiplication sign is a reminder to pay attention to both sides. It is not a formula that calculates a precise amount of freedom.

Higher earnings can create room to build alternatives, but only if some of that room is preserved. More alternatives can make it easier to choose where your skills are most useful. Neither side removes uncertainty or the obligations that shape a real life.

Two questions to revisit: What am I getting better at that someone will value? What am I building that gives me a more credible choice about what comes next?

First make your time valuable, then make it yours.

Real decisions, with the arithmetic shown

One real decision. Three lenses. One verdict. One number.

The people who advise you on careers do not talk about money, and the people who advise you on money do not talk about careers. Most of the decisions that shape a senior life sit exactly between the two. So each case here runs one real decision through both engines and reads off what is left: Earning Power, Exit Power, and how many acceptable choices the person has afterwards.

The pay cut, again

He is 42, a Director at a large company everyone has heard of. ₹1 crore of total cash: ₹85 lakh fixed, ₹15 lakh bonus, with ₹35 lakh of unvested RSUs on top. The offer is ₹70 lakh cash, a 30 percent cut, plus 0.4 percent of a much smaller company. The work is the work he says he wants. At the big firm he runs a slice of a function and somebody above him makes the call. At the small firm he owns the function and reports to the CEO. Home loan. Two children in school. His spouse works.

One number is usually missing from this conversation, so let me put it in first. His liquid savings are ₹35 lakh. His household costs ₹3.4 lakh a month.

=
10months of runway

Earning Power

Not close. Staying produces three more years of evidence for a thing he has already proved. Going produces the thing the market buys above Director: owning a function, reporting to a CEO, making calls and carrying them. The risk is real and named: if the small company fails badly, Director at a firm everyone knows prices better than VP at a firm nobody has heard of. Real, and not fatal.

Says go

Exit Power

Runway before: ten months. Runway after: ten months, because a new job changes neither the loan nor the school fees. That is where the analysis usually stops, and it is wrong. On ₹1 crore he adds about ₹2.1 lakh a month, a new month of runway every seven weeks. On ₹70 lakh he adds about ₹60,000, a new month every six months. He is not losing runway. He is losing the ability to build it. And his salary and his equity now fail together, in the same quarter, for the same reason.

Says no

Optionality

Before: stay, move to a similar firm, take this offer if the money worked, wait for something better. After: stay at the small company, and hope. At ten months and a savings rate near zero he has taken a job where he cannot afford a bad year, at a company where bad years are more likely, holding an asset he cannot sell.

Falls

Verdict: not yet

And not yet is a date. About eighteen months of hard saving at his current salary, ₹2.1 lakh a month, puts him near ₹75 lakh liquid, around twenty-two months of runway. At that point he can absorb a 30 percent cut, sit at a low savings rate for three years, and still have room to be wrong. His current job, from today, is not a cage. It is the funding round for the move he actually wants.

This specific offer will not wait eighteen months, and he will feel that as a loss. Two things about that. If the ladder position is really the problem, this is not the last company that needs somebody to own a function. And an offer is not a fixed object: more cash and less equity, a signing amount that closes part of the gap, a guaranteed first-year bonus. Most people never try, because they treat the offer as the decision.

The number that changes it

Take the identical man. Same age, same offer, same company, same ₹3.4 lakh a month. Give him ₹1.4 crore in liquid savings instead of ₹35 lakh. Forty-one months. Now the slow accumulation does not matter, the equity being worth nothing is affordable, and a company failure is a bad year rather than a catastrophe. Verdict: take it, and not close. Same offer, same ladder, same man. One number different, and the answer reverses. That number appears nowhere in the offer letter and would not come up in any career conversation either man is likely to have.

On the equity, since you are wondering: 0.4 percent of a company that eventually sells for ₹1,000 crore is ₹4 crore, and that is the reason people take these jobs. Most companies do not get there, and more importantly the illiquid upside cannot pay for the liquid downside. It cannot pay school fees in year two or fund a job search. Write it down to zero on the day you sign. If the decision does not survive that, the decision is not sound.

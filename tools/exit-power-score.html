The Exit Power Score

Exit Power is how long you could decline income and still be yourself, and how many acceptable options exist if the income stops. It is not net worth. It is the part that is liquid, available and yours, measured against what your life actually costs.

Five questions. Nothing is sent anywhere until you choose to send it. If you do not know an answer, that itself is information worth sitting with.

If your salary stopped tomorrow, how long could your household run on what you can actually reach within a fortnight?

What share of your household net worth sits in your employer's stock, vested and unvested together?

What share of household income comes from your one employer?

Add up your fixed monthly commitments that cannot be paused: loan payments, school fees, rent, support for parents, insurance. What share of take-home pay is that?

If you had to find something new, how strong is your position outside your current company?

Answer every question to see your reading.

Strong. You have real room. The interesting question for you is no longer safety, it is what you intend to do with the room, because most people at this level never actually use it. Solid. You could survive a shock and decline something you did not want. The usual weak spot at this level is concentration rather than runway. Check what share of your future sits with one employer. Thin. You would get through a layoff but you are not in a position to take a risk on purpose, which means your career decisions are being made by your balance sheet rather than by you. Trapped earner. This is the most common result among people who look successful from outside, and it is usually a surprise to them. Nothing here is a judgement on how well you have done. It is a description of how little room you left yourself while doing it. Fragile. Worth treating as urgent rather than embarrassing. Runway first, before anything else on the list.

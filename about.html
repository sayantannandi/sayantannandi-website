The senior colleague who did the maths on his own life, and showed his working.

That is the job of this site, and it is the only claim I make for myself. Not that I solved anything. That I ran the audit, wrote the arithmetic down, and found most of it applied to people I had worked alongside for years without any of us ever saying so.

Safe

For about three years I was the person my boss never had to worry about. Things I owned did not go wrong. I did not escalate. I did not bring problems. In conversations about me, somebody always said the word safe, and I took it as a compliment for a very long time.

Then a role I wanted went to somebody else, and the reason given was that he was seen as more strategic. I decided it was politics and worked harder, which is what everybody does with that sentence.

What I worked out much later, reading my own old emails, is that my updates confirmed nothing new had gone wrong. They never once took an existing worry off anybody's list. Those are two different jobs, and I had been doing the smaller one, very well, for three years. The rules of a career had changed on me and nobody had sent a note. Most of what I now teach about Earning Power is the note I wish somebody had sent.

Eight months

Years later I had the best year of my career. A big raise, the title, something good said about me in a room I was not in. And a few months after that I sat in a meeting where a decision was going wrong, with the right number in my notebook, and said nothing, because somewhere behind my face a sum ran about what disagreeing would cost.

That evening I ran a different sum. What I had, divided by what my life cost each month. Eight months. After a year like that. And when I went back three years, income up a lot, runway barely moved. Every raise had gone somewhere defensible. Together they meant I had spent three years getting much better paid and had not bought one additional month of freedom.

That is where the second half of the argument comes from. Not from a theory about money. From noticing that a well-paid man had rebuilt the exact condition he grew up in, solvent and one problem away from trouble, at ten times the income, and that it had produced the same silence in a meeting.

What I do now

I spent fifteen years in technology leadership, with an MBA from ISB somewhere in the middle, and eventually reached a point where I could step away and ask what a successful career is for. I write a weekly letter, I teach the twelve behaviours that separate a senior technologist from a business leader, and I run real decisions through both halves of the arithmetic for people who ask. Two books are in progress. A lot of what I write starts in a conversation with someone stuck on a decision that has a career half and a money half, so if that is you, write.

What I am not

Not a coach in the generic sense, not a finance influencer, and not selling escape. I like work. I am not a SEBI-registered investment adviser, and nothing here is personalised financial advice. The sums are simple and the point of them is that you run them on your own numbers. Reading about someone else's runway is interesting. Knowing your own changes what you say in meetings.

The Optional Career

One letter a week. The rules of a senior career change without telling you. Each issue takes one of those changes, one real situation and one number, and works out what to do. About a thousand words. Read the latest issue and the promise before you join, if you like.

No sequence of seven emails about the course. One letter a week, and the occasional note when something on this site changes. Leave in one click.

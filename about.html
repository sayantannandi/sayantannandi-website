About

I moved up quickly, and still had eight months of runway.

It took me years to see that a career has two numbers. I write and teach about both.

At every company I worked for, I was put on the career acceleration program. I was promoted in almost every cycle.

At 33, after a raise of about 40 percent, I sat in a meeting, saw a decision going the wrong way, and said nothing. That evening I divided what we had by what our household cost each month. The answer was eight months.

So I started working on two things at once: what I could earn, and how long we could go without it.

The first half I learned from the other side of the table, in the meetings where promotion lists get made. Hard work hardly came up. Everyone being discussed worked hard. What separated the names that moved up from the names that stayed was a small set of skills: how a person explained what their work had changed, how they made a call without all the facts, and how they got help from people who did not report to them.

It was the same wherever I worked. I have been inside global giants such as Schneider Electric and Novartis, and inside Flipkart, one of India's internet giants. The three had little in common. The skills that marked out the people who moved up were the same in all of them.

Nobody taught those skills. My MBA at ISB gave me a very talented set of peers. It did not teach these.

So I began teaching them to my own teams. Now I teach them to anyone who wants them.

Over those same years the eight months became more than 250. That is over twenty years in which I do not have to sell my time to an employer. I call that Exit Power. What I chose to do with it is write and teach full time, about both halves.

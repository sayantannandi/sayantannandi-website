Earning power

Develop the contribution
your next role needs.

Earning power is your ability to earn from capabilities that other people value. Your current salary describes one agreement. The work you can take on next helps shape the agreements still available to you.

My teaching focuses on a particular transition: experienced professionals growing into leadership. That means taking responsibility for decisions and results that reach beyond your own task list.

The work changes before the title does.

Early in a career, reliable execution can distinguish you. You learn the systems, solve difficult problems and deliver with less supervision. Later, many of the people around you can do those things too. A wider role asks another question: what can move forward because you are responsible for it?

More output may leave that question unanswered.

A person who resolves every difficult issue can become a queue everyone else waits in. A person who understands the pattern behind those issues may be able to remove a recurring cause. Both contributions require expertise. The second also requires judgement about priorities and the ability to get a change adopted by other people.

Technical depth needs a business explanation.

You do not have to abandon your expertise to participate in business decisions. You need to explain what the detail changes. A faster process may reduce a delay for customers. A system failure may put a payment deadline at risk. A proposed fix may require another team to postpone something important.

The explanation lets someone compare consequences. It also makes the limits of the evidence visible, which matters when your words may become another person’s commitment.

What greater contribution
looks like in practice.

These are learnable responses to familiar workplace situations. They need practice and feedback in the work itself.

Explain the outcome.

Show what changed for a customer, team or business. Separate the result you observed from the improvement you still expect, and name other people’s contribution.

Make a recommendation.

State the decision, the evidence and the condition that would change your view. Incomplete information may require a smaller commitment or an earlier review.

Give ownership room to work.

Delegation needs clarity about authority and the points that require escalation. A task has not fully moved if every meaningful choice still needs your approval.

Work across competing priorities.

A colleague may have a reasonable reason to resist your request. Understanding their constraints makes it possible to propose an exchange they can actually support.

Illustrative workplace example

The same work, explained differently.

The activity update

“We completed the pilot and improved processing time by 30%.”

The recommendation

“The pilot used 28 staff-hours, compared with 40 for the previous cycle. I recommend one more cycle at higher volume before expanding it. Staffing costs have not changed, so we should report time released rather than a cash saving.”

The second version helps a leader make a decision. It preserves the useful result while identifying what remains untested. It also avoids turning twelve hours into a financial claim the evidence cannot support.

Evidence makes development easier to assess.

A promotion conversation needs examples of responsibility, alongside effort. The useful detail includes the decision you owned, the trade-off you considered and what happened afterwards. A course can help you practise and assemble that evidence. The availability of a role and the employer’s assessment still affect the outcome.

Leadership is one route to earning well. Specialist careers and other forms of work can also create value. The question is which contribution you want to develop and where it is needed.

What the income makes possible.

More valuable work can strengthen your earning power. The commitments and choices made around that income determine how much flexibility it creates. That is the connection to exit power.

The wider philosophy holds both questions together. The Optional Career explores them through workplace situations and the decisions that follow a working day.

Growth becomes more useful when you can describe both the work it opens up and the life it supports.

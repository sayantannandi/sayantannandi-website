The problem

Two ways to get stuck

Most managers who feel stuck are stuck in one of two places. At work, at a level they have outgrown. Or at home, where every raise is already spent. Many are stuck in both.

1

The ceiling

Each level pays for something different. Most people keep doing what the last level paid for, because it is what got them promoted.

2

The trap: pay doubles, freedom does not

Every raise finds a home: a bigger flat, a car, a better holiday. Pay goes up, costs climb right behind it, and the months you could last without the salary quietly fall.

The fix is one rule, set before the raise lands: decide how much of it is allowed to become a new cost. See the working →

High Earning Power, low Exit Power

Trapped Earner

Paid well, with little room to move.

You are good at your job and paid well. But each raise turned into a bigger EMI or a better holiday, so you could last only a few months without the salary. You say yes in meetings because a no feels too costly.

Work on first: keep the pay, and move part of every raise out on salary day.

High on both

Optional Career

Worth a lot, and free to choose.

Your work is worth a lot, and you have saved enough months to match. You can push for the bigger role, turn down a bad one, or slow down for a year. Each choice is one you can afford.

Work on first: protect it. New commitments quietly eat the months.

Low on both

Exposed on both sides

No cushion, and no clear next step.

Your pay has stopped growing and little is saved. A reorg or a new boss hits hard here, because there is no cushion at home and no clear next step at work.

Work on first: start at work. One habit from the level below is usually what holds you back.

Low Earning Power, high Exit Power

Safe, and standing still

Saved well, stalled at work.

You have saved well, but your value at work has stopped rising. You can afford to wait, so it is easy to keep waiting. Money is not the problem here. The next level is.

Work on first: use the cushion to take the stretch role you would not have risked before.

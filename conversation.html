Twenty minutes, on a call

A conversation about your situation.

Not a sales call, and not coaching. Twenty minutes on where you are, what has stopped working, and whether the December cohort fits it. If it does not, I will tell you, and I will tell you what I think would.

What it is

You describe the situation. I ask the questions I would ask a colleague over coffee: what the last piece of feedback actually said, what your updates look like, what you are being asked to decide. Then I give you a plain view. Sometimes that is "the cohort is built for exactly this." Sometimes it is "you do not need eight weeks, you need one sentence rewritten, here it is." Both happen, and I am fine with either.

Who it is for

Senior Manager to Director, twelve or more years in. Technology, data, product or transformation, usually in a GCC, a product company or IT services. The common thread is that the work is still good and something around it has changed: the promotion did not come, the word strategic has been used about somebody else, or the update you are proudest of got no reply.

If you are earlier in your career, or in a different function, the free diagnostics on Start here will do more for you than this call, and they will do it today.

What to bring

One of two things. The last update you sent your boss, as sent, not tidied. Or the piece of feedback you cannot decode, in the words it was given. Either one tells me more in a minute than a CV does in ten. You do not need to prepare anything else.

Pick a time

Slots are on weekday mornings, Indian time. If none of them work, write to me and we will find one.

Book twenty minutes

Nothing is recorded. Nothing from the call goes into a list. If I mention a situation like yours in writing later, it will be unrecognisable and I will have asked.

First make your time valuable. Then make it yours.

Careers, money, and how they combine into choices. Written for senior professionals in India who are doing well on paper and want more say over what comes next.
